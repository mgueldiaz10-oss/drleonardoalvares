Division 01 · Coherent Light Source

High Resolution Laser Lab

The light source of the ultra-high resolution UV spectrometer comes from this lab. In here, there is a set of configurated laser cavities which allows us to get highly monochromatic UV photons with the maximum power (according to the frequency region and the operator´s aligning skills).

Optical table and high-resolution laser cavities assembly
Spectra Physics Millennia pump laser (5W, 532 nm)

Our initial laser cavity is a Spectra Physics Millennia diode laser, configurated to provide 5W of output power at the second harmonic (532 nm). Such laser generates the pump light to the next laser cavity which is a Spectra Physics Matisse DS.

The Matisse DS is a ring dye laser cavity; where, the Millennia radiation excites the chilled circulating dye, which fluoresces at a specific frequency range. Moreover, this laser cavity is digitally frequency adjustable, manual and computer (fine tuning) optimized and scanned in a strongly narrow frequency range (about 1 cm⁻¹ in the fundamental visible region). All this can be achieved thanks to the attached Fabry Perot interferometer (reference station) and to all the optical components within the laser cavity. The visible laser light beam generated by this laser is of about 250 kHz broad.

Fabry-Perot interferometer and reference station
Spectra Physics Matisse DS ring dye laser cavity

The narrow broadband laser beam is then beam-splitted to mainly a Spectra Physics Wavetrain; where, through non linear optics the Second Harmonic of the fundamental light is generated. Therefore, highly monochromatic UV photons are produced.

Spectra Physics Wavetrain Second Harmonic Generation (SHG) module in operation
Division 02 · Vacuum Physics & Supersonic Expansion

Molecular Beam Machine

The molecular beam machine is a stainless-steel differential vacuum chamber. It is a three attached vacuum chambers; each connected to its corresponding vacuum diffusion pump. They are interconnected throughout micrometric holes manually controlled by mechanical valves. The first (source) chamber, where a quartz nozzle is allocated to generate the molecular sample in the gas phase, can achieve about 10⁻⁵ Torr. The next (buffer) chamber may be able to achieve about 10⁻⁶ Torr. And the last (research) chamber might be able to achieve 10⁻⁷ Torr; since, liquid nitrogen may be used as a cryogenic fluid to enhance its vacuum conditions.

Three-stage stainless steel differential ultra-high vacuum chamber

Everyone of the diffusion pumps works with a special silicone oil, which is heated up by adapted electrical resistances to form a laminar flow inside, condensed by chilled water provided by an adapted water chiller.

Silicone oil diffusion vacuum pumping assembly
Cryogenic vacuum piping and micrometric valves

Everyone of the chambers is connected through a common line to a mechanical vacuum machine provided with a booster to enhance its operation conditions. Such mechanical vacuum system may achieve about 10⁻² Torr; necessary as a preliminary vacuum level in order to get started each one of the three diffusion pumps attached to every chamber.

Mechanical pump with vacuum booster for preliminary roughing
Molecular interaction and laser fluorescence detection zone

Moreover, the aligning procedure of the quartz nozzle plays an important role on succeeding getting molecular signal out of the beam machine; since, the micrometric hole at the tip of the nozzle needs to be centered towards another micrometric hole in a metallic skimmer to pass the molecular beam to the next vacuum chamber, and so on. Therefore, the molecular beam might be, eventually, perpendicularly crossed by the laser UV beam to excite the molecular system to be analyzed thereafter.

Division 03 · Theoretical Simulation & Quantum Mechanics

Molecular Quantum Computing

The molecular quantum computing is a simulation laboratory, which enables us to build our molecules and/or molecular clusters under study in the molecular spectroscopy lab.

Once the molecular systems are built, they should be geometry optimized (energy minimized) in terms of their structural parameters, such as, bond lengths, bond angles, dihedral angles, intermolecular interactions and so on. To do this, quantum mechanical calculations should be performed, under either a commercial software (Gaussian 16) or a home-made program (Deamon). On such environment, the user should pick an approximate theoretical quantum mechanical method and a numerical basis set; which might be accurate enough to predict a good set of inertial parameters to be used as the initial set of rotational constants to build the rigid or pseudo rigid rotor Hamiltonians, both in S₀ and S₁ electronic states.

Computational workstation cluster for rotational Hamiltonian fitting (JB95 / ASYM40)

Besides of the theoretical prediction of the inertial parameters described above, the quantum mechanical calculations might provide much more molecular information that is of interest on describing the absorption of a photon of UV light by the molecular system. These data include vibrational frequencies, molecular point group, frequency activity in the infrared, energy estimates of the excited electronic states, formal charges distribution, dipole moment orientation, molecular orbital populations, thermochemical information, etc.

Moreover, this technique is also exploited on the photocatalysis experiments, by estimation of the frequency vibrational modes of reactants, and elucidating throughout a reaction coordinate (low frequency mode) to be vibronically excited with our UV laser system in the condensed phase.

Division 04 · Applied Photochemistry & Environmental Remediation

Photocatalysis and Photodegradation Techniques

In this division, the interaction between light and matter is harnessed to address environmental challenges through advanced photocatalytic processes. The research focuses on the degradation of persistent organic pollutants, micro-contaminants, and industrial dyes using tailored semiconductor photocatalysts activated under controlled UV and laser radiation.

Photochemical reactor and controlled irradiation instrumentation
Degradation kinetics monitoring and spectrophotometric analytical bench

Reaction kinetics and photochemical milestones are monitored in real time using spectrophotometric and fluorescence tracking. By coupling experimental degradation profiles with theoretical vibrational frequency simulations, our researchers determine the optimal vibronic excitation pathways that accelerate bond cleavage in toxic molecules, advancing green chemistry and water purification technologies.

Division 05 · Academic Technical Staff

Lab Technician

Dr. Eduardo Montes Ramírez

Professional Academic Technician A · SNI Level I
  • Institutional Category: Professional Academic Technician A
  • National System of Researchers: Level I (SNI I)
  • Total Scientific Production: 25 international papers, >230 direct citations
  • Supervised Theses: 2 B.Sc., 2 M.Sc., 1 Ph.D.
  • Email Address: montes.e@ugto.mx

Graduated from the National Polytechnic Institute (ESIME-IPN) in Mexico City in 2005 as an Industrial Robotics Engineer. He worked for 4 years as a product engineer in the HVAC industry. He pursued graduate studies at the Center for Applied Science and Advanced Technology (CICATA-IPN), obtaining his M.Sc. (2011) and Ph.D. (2015) in Advanced Technology with Honors (Mención Honorífica). His doctoral research focused on the synthesis, spectroscopic characterization, and applications of rare-earth doped metal oxide phosphors.

He completed postdoctoral research fellowships at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, Spain (2017) and at the Centro de Investigaciones en Óptica (CIO, 2018), focusing on phosphors for biological applications and developing advanced optical instrumentation. In 2020, he joined the Division of Sciences and Engineering at the University of Guanajuato as adjunct faculty, and in 2023 was appointed Professional Academic Technician overseeing the Ultra-High Resolution Spectroscopy and Photocatalysis Laboratories at DCI-UG.

He received the 2012 Research Award in Basic Research from the National Polytechnic Institute. He has been a member of the National System of Researchers (SNI) since 2017, currently holding Level I distinction. At DCI-UG, he has led and collaborated on numerous competitive research grants funded by DAIP-UG, advised undergraduate and graduate students, and authored over 25 international peer-reviewed publications with more than 230 direct citations.
